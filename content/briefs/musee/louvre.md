## Primary task

Help a visitor decide **what kind of Louvre visit to build**, rather than encouraging an impossible exhaustive checklist. Answer current practical questions early enough to prevent a bad visit.

## Visit proposition

The Louvre is the right choice for an encyclopedic visit across multiple civilizations and media, provided the visitor selects a coherent axis/departments before arriving and checks current room access.

## Audience / context

Visitors already considering the Louvre, often first-time or occasional museum visitors, who need a practical selection method. Do not invent personas, crowd tolerance or cultural expertise.

## Ownership and exclusions

This page owns the exact Louvre entity, palace/collection identity, current practical visit and Louvre-specific route selection.

Do not own:
- detailed temporary-exhibition coverage (`/expositions/` owner);
- generic Paris museum comparison (`/musees/paris/` owner);
- generic museum-preparation methodology (`/guides/comment-preparer-visite-musee/` owner).

## MUST

- explain the selection problem created by eight departments / scale;
- give a concrete route-selection framework without pretending one route is universally best;
- current hours / Tuesday closure;
- current 2026 admission bands and reservation guidance;
- warn that room access and work visibility can change;
- link official room-access and itinerary tools;
- preserve collection != current-display distinction.

## SHOULD

- explain why the palace affects navigation and visit expectations;
- offer several possible axes (civilizations, painting, palace/objects, etc.) without forcing exactly three;
- compare briefly with Orsay or Louvre-Lens only when it helps the choice.

## OPTIONAL

- accessibility/family detail only when current and decision-relevant;
- named masterpieces only if current visibility is verified and they serve the route logic.

## Structure for this page — not a template

1. Decision answer: the Louvre requires selection.
2. Quick practical facts.
3. **Choose your route** — a flexible list / decision block, not a three-card “reasons” grid.
4. Palace/navigation context.
5. Current visit constraints: hours, prices, reservation, room calendar.
6. Relevant next links.

The official Louvre itinerary catalogue can be referenced as proof that themed/time-bounded routes are normal. Do not convert those examples into a universal site-wide recommended duration.

## Facts / freshness

VOLATILE: hours, prices, reservation, room access, entrances.
STABLE: palace role, department structure, broad collection scope.

Current official facts were rechecked 2026-09-14.

## Entities

Musée du Louvre; Palais du Louvre; eight conservation departments; official itinerary system; official room-access calendar.

## Anti-patterns

- “10 chefs-d’œuvre incontournables” checklist as page spine;
- fixed “3 ways / 3 reasons” because the component exists;
- invented best visiting time or ideal duration;
- guarantee that a famous work is currently visible;
- encyclopedic palace history unrelated to planning.

## Success criteria

The visitor can answer: **why Louvre, which axis, what must I check today?** The structure should visibly differ from Orsay and from other museum pages while keeping shared UI components where useful.
